# 2.5. Time-Saving Subnetting Tricks

## 1. Introduction
The 7-step process for subnetting is reliable, but for larger target IP addresses, listing increments from `0` can be slow. This note introduces four powerful speed tips that allow you to jump much closer to your target IP, dramatically reducing the time it takes to solve problems.

These tricks are shortcuts for Step 3 of our process: "List Network IDs."

---

## 2. Speed Tip 1: Multiply, Double, and Triple

When your target IP is a large number, you don't need to start counting your increments from `0`. You can jump to a much closer starting point.

**The Trick:**
1.  Take the **Group Size** and multiply it by **10**. This gives you a convenient, higher starting point.
2.  If needed, you can **double or triple** that result to get even closer.
3.  From that new starting point, continue incrementing by the original **Group Size**.

#### Example: `10.3.3.170 /29`

- **Group Size:** 8
- **Target IP:** `.170`

Instead of `0, 8, 16, 24...`:
1.  **Multiply by 10:** `8 * 10 = 80`.
2.  **Double the result:** `80 * 2 = 160`. This is very close to `170`.
3.  **Start incrementing from 160 by the original group size (8):**
    `160, 168, 176`

We've passed our target `.170`. The range is between `168` and `176`.
- **Network ID:** `10.3.3.168`
- **Next Network:** `10.3.3.176`
- **Broadcast IP:** `10.3.3.175`
- ...and so on.

This saved us from listing out 20 increments.

---

## 3. Speed Tip 2: The Magic Number 128

For any group size in the cheat sheet, the increments will *always* land on `128` at some point.

**The Trick:**
If your target IP is greater than `128`, you can **always use `128` as a starting point** for your increments, regardless of the group size.

#### Example: `10.3.3.147 /28`

- **Group Size:** 16
- **Target IP:** `.147`

Instead of counting from 0, we see that `147` is greater than `128`.
1.  **Start at 128.**
2.  **Increment by the group size (16):**
    `128, 144, 160`

We've passed our target `.147`. The range is between `144` and `160`.
- **Network ID:** `10.3.3.144`
- **Next Network:** `10.3.3.160`
- **Broadcast IP:** `10.3.3.159`

---

## 4. Speed Tip 3: Jump to Subnet Mask Values

This is one of the most powerful tricks. Every group size will eventually land on the subnet mask values from its own column and every column to its left.

**The Trick:**
Find the subnet mask value in the cheat sheet that is closest to (but not over) your target IP. You can use that value as your starting point for increments.

#### Example: `10.3.3.197 /30`

- **Group Size:** 4
- **Target IP:** `.197`
- **Cheat Sheet Mask Row:** `128, 192, 224, 240, 248, 252...`

1.  Look for the largest mask value that is less than `197`. That value is `192`.
2.  **Start your increments from 192.**
3.  **Increment by the group size (4):**
    `192, 196, 200`

We've passed our target `.197`. The range is between `196` and `200`.
- **Network ID:** `10.3.3.196`
- **Next Network:** `10.3.3.200`
- **Broadcast IP:** `10.3.3.199`

---

## 5. Speed Tip 4: Work Backwards (Decrementing)

Sometimes your target IP is just below one of the convenient starting points from the other speed tips (like 128 or 192).

**The Trick:**
Start at a higher, convenient number (like 128, 192, or 256) and **subtract (decrement)** by the group size until you pass your target IP going backward.

#### Example: `10.3.3.117 /29`

- **Group Size:** 8
- **Target IP:** `.117`

Starting at 0 would be slow. The closest mask value is `192` (too high) or `128`. `128` is a good starting point.
1.  **Start at 128.**
2.  **Decrement by the group size (8):**
    `128, 120, 112`

We've now gone past our target `.117` in the reverse direction. This means our IP lives in the block *between* `112` and `120`.
- **Network ID:** `10.3.3.112`
- **Next Network:** `10.3.3.120`
- **Broadcast IP:** `10.3.3.119`

---

## Summary of Tricks

| Tip | When to Use | How it Works |
| :-- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Multiply** | Target IP is large (e.g., > 80). | Multiply group size by 10, then double/triple to get close. |
| **2. Jump to 128** | Target IP is > 128. | Always start increments from 128. |
| **3. Jump to Mask**| Any target IP. | Find the largest mask value less than the target and start there. |
| **4. Decrement** | Target IP is just below a convenient starting point. | Start high (e.g., at 128 or 256) and subtract the group size. |

By combining these tricks, you can solve any subnetting problem in the fourth octet with incredible speed.
