
## 1. Definition
**VLSM (Variable Length Subnet Mask)** allows a network engineer to use different subnet masks for different subnets within the same major network. It essentially "subnets a subnet."

*   **Goal:** Minimize IP address wastage.
*   **Requirement:** The routing protocol must support it (RIPv2, OSPF, EIGRP). RIPv1 *cannot* do VLSM.

## 2. The Golden Rule of VLSM
To apply VLSM correctly, you must **sort your requirements from largest to smallest**.
1.  Assign the subnet for the largest group of hosts first.
2.  Assign the next largest group.
3.  ...
4.  Assign the WAN links (usually /30 or /31) last.

## 3. Practical Exercise (From Slide 80)
**Scenario:** Network `192.168.10.0/24`. We need subnets for:
*   Dept 2: 100 hosts
*   Dept 1: 57 hosts
*   Dept 4: 25 hosts
*   Dept 3: 12 hosts

### Step 1: Dept 2 (100 hosts)
*   Need: $2^h - 2 \ge 100 \rightarrow 2^7 = 128$. (7 host bits).
*   Mask: $32 - 7 = /25$ (`255.255.255.128`).
*   **Subnet:** `192.168.10.0/25`
*   Range: `.0` to `.127`.

### Step 2: Dept 1 (57 hosts)
*   Next available IP starts at `.128`.
*   Need: $2^h - 2 \ge 57 \rightarrow 2^6 = 64$. (6 host bits).
*   Mask: $32 - 6 = /26$ (`255.255.255.192`).
*   **Subnet:** `192.168.10.128/26`
*   Range: `.128` to `.191` (128 + 64 = 192).

### Step 3: Dept 4 (25 hosts)
*   Next available IP starts at `.192`.
*   Need: $2^h - 2 \ge 25 \rightarrow 2^5 = 32$. (5 host bits).
*   Mask: $32 - 5 = /27$ (`255.255.255.224`).
*   **Subnet:** `192.168.10.192/27`
*   Range: `.192` to `.223`.

### Step 4: Dept 3 (12 hosts)
*   Next available IP starts at `.224`.
*   Need: $2^h - 2 \ge 12 \rightarrow 2^4 = 16$. (4 host bits).
*   Mask: $32 - 4 = /28$ (`255.255.255.240`).
*   **Subnet:** `192.168.10.224/28`
*   Range: `.224` to `.239`.

> [!WARNING] **Limitation**
> While efficient, VLSM creates a complex addressing plan. If not documented well, it creates "fragmentation" where small gaps of unused IPs are scattered and unusable.
