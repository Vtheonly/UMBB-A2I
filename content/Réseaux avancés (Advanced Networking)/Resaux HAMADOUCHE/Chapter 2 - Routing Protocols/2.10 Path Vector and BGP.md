
# 2.10. External Routing Protocols (BGP)

### **Context: The Protocol of the Internet**
When we connect different Autonomous Systems (e.g., Google to Verizon to Your University), we cannot use OSPF or RIP.
1.  **Scale:** The Internet routing table has nearly 1 million routes. OSPF cannot handle an LSDB that size.
2.  **Policy:** Routing between companies isn't just about speed; it's about business contracts. "I will route traffic for you, but only if you pay me."

### **BGP (Border Gateway Protocol)**
BGP is the only current EGP used on the Internet. It is classified as a **Path Vector** protocol.

*   **Path Vector:** Instead of a simple metric (hops), BGP sends the **full list of AS numbers** that a packet must traverse to reach the destination (e.g., Path: `AS100 -> AS200 -> AS300`).
*   **Loop Prevention:** If a router sees its own AS number in the path list, it drops the update (it knows the packet has looped).

### **Characteristics**
1.  **Reliability:** Uses **TCP Port 179**. Because it uses TCP, it doesn't need its own fragmentation or retransmission mechanisms; TCP handles that.
2.  **Policy-Based:** Decisions are not made purely on metrics. An admin can manually set "Local Preference" or "MED" attributes to force traffic one way or another based on business agreements.
3.  **Updates:** Only sends **triggered updates** (changes only). No periodic floods.

### **BGP Messages**
1.  **Open:** Starts the session between neighbors (peers). Includes ASN and Hold Time.
2.  **Update:** The meat of the protocol. Announces new routes or withdraws dead routes.
3.  **Keepalive:** Sent periodically to keep the TCP session open (prevents the Hold Timer from expiring).
4.  **Notification:** Sent when an error occurs (e.g., bad configuration). The connection closes immediately after this.

### **BGP Speaker Types**
*   **eBGP (External BGP):** Session between routers in **different** ASs.
*   **iBGP (Internal BGP):** Session between routers in the **same** AS. Used to carry external routes *across* a transit AS.

> [!TIP] **Why not just OSPF everywhere?**
> Imagine if Google's internal link flapped and every router in the world had to recalculate Dijkstra. The internet would collapse.
> BGP hides these internal fluctuations. It says "I can reach Google," regardless of the internal details of Google's network. Stability > Speed.
